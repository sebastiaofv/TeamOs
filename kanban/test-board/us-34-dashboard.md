# US-34 — Project Overview redesign

Open `us-9-sections.board.json`, select its project, and open Dashboard (last button in the main navigation). Open the calendar/date range field and set Start date to 24/10/2026 and End date to 31/10/2026.

1. Overview and Sprint Analytics sit in the next navigation row. On the right, the compact Visible sections icon precedes the calendar/date range; its field shows both dates separated by a dash. Escape and clicking outside close either menu. No visible Dates label or Snapshot dropdown is present; the information icon explains reporting rules.
2. Project Overview is the only section. On desktop, project progress and phases occupy the first row; Milestones, Sprint and Epics share the next row. Workitem Status, Activity Summary and Workitem details occupy the following row, in that order. Open risks, deliverables and decisions remain below. Activity Summary and Workitem details are visible by default; the four independent lists are opt-in. Saved visibility/order choices remain shared and Reset to Default restores these defaults.
3. Project progress shows 31% inside a circular chart, a thin divider and 4 / 13 workitems on its right. Cancelled work and parent workitems are excluded; archived leaves remain included. Blocked workitems are absent. Click the indicator to inspect sources.
4. Active phases shows all six parent phases chronologically in a connected horizontal sequence. API delivery and UX delivery are children and absent. Completed parent phase is green with a check; Delivery programme has an inner blue circle; future unstarted phases have blue outlines. Late/Overdue alerts take priority and highlight the missed start/end date in red. Percentages still aggregate descendant work and respect deliverable acceptance gates. Names open the Roadmap; percentages open sources. The diagram wraps into rows on smaller screens; the card grows to show every phase without internal scrolling, and adjacent cards stretch to match. Circles remain round in Classic and Modern; phase names occupy two lines with ellipsis and full hover text.
5. Milestones shows icon-only totals for completed, delayed and all remaining checkpoints. The widget is about 30% wider than the preceding revision, with name, date and progress on one line. The compact list contains the latest recorded completion through its tenth day, every overdue checkpoint and the next pending checkpoint, sorted by due date. The report end date controls the completion window; planned due dates are never used as completion dates. Completed baselines without a recorded completion date count in the summary but are omitted from the recent list. If there are no relevant rows, a quiet empty message appears. Completed milestones have green bars with no percentage; overdue milestones show a warning icon beside the date without a status column. Empty denominators remain N/A. Hover long names and open the milestone detail. Deliverable acceptance gates still apply.
6. Sprint shows its truncated name and dd/mm dates on the first line; its On Target/Completed/Overdue badge and three compact indicators share the second line. Its fixed height matches Epics and fits two Sprints before internal scrolling. Workitems opens the shared detail with the Sprint name as its title. It includes Sprints active at the end date and those completed within the inclusive selected period, using recorded closure dates and frozen metrics when available; legacy completed Sprints fall back to their calendar end date. Several Sprints may appear. It retains planning-unit completion (0%). Its three indicators show 2 Workitems, 0 / 0 Story Points and 0% Progress for this legacy fixture. Workitems include Stories, independent Tasks and Bugs; dependent Tasks do not duplicate counts or points. Completion points reuse development completion. Explicitly active overdue Sprints remain visible; empty projects show a discreet message.
7. Workitem status has one aligned horizontal row each for User Stories, independent Tasks and Bugs, aggregated by workflow category with a common category palette, continuous segments without gaps, counts inside sufficiently wide segments, percentages in hover details and one shared legend. Independent Tasks total 9; child Tasks under Stories/Bugs are excluded and Tasks directly under Epics remain independent. There are no source buttons on these bars and no empty source section below the Dashboard; all progress drill-downs use the integrated Workitem details widget when selected; no bottom list is ever rendered. Activity selections update the shared detail widget in place.
8. Epics has compact rows without headers: small ID above a truncated title, inline SP label and completed/total points and a wide, thick progress bar with the rounded percentage to its right. The fixed-height widget scrolls internally for many Epics. Points reuse planning-unit estimates and development completion, including direct Tasks and Bugs while excluding dependent Tasks; historical consultation uses the selected hierarchy and estimates. Progress bars are thicker and green at 100%, with the percentage hidden on completion. Delivery epic shows 0% and its title has more room without a status column. Unstarted/cancelled Epics are omitted; completed Epics remain included. ID and title open the normal workitem detail, editable under the same project permissions as Table and Board.
9. The date range controls historical snapshots and activity. Start affects period activity; status widgets follow End. Dates before the baseline show N/A, invalid periods show the existing error and historical consultation continues through the end-date filter. Sprint Analytics retains its contents and interactions.
10. Repeat in English/Portuguese, light/dark and at 390px width. Check popover keyboard access, internal scrolling, long titles and no page overflow. Consultation must not change the project file. Activity layout persistence, reopening/reset and read-only behavior remain covered by [activity scenarios](issue-83-progress.md).
